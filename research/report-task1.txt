The application uses Open-Meteo's Global Flood API, distributing Copernicus CEMS GloFAS modelled daily river discharge in m³/s. Both history and forecast request seamless_v4, nearest-cell selection and GMT dates. Fields are daily.time, daily.river_discharge, daily_units and returned coordinates. Forecast queries cover today and the following two days. These are public model estimates, not gauge observations; seamless history can combine reanalysis and archived forecasts [4].

Open-Meteo data is CC BY 4.0 and the free endpoint permits this educational use. OPW station coordinates and river identities are credited under CC BY 4.0. Met Éireann warnings use its custom open-data licence: original warning content must remain intact and current. Exact URLs, fields, retrieval times and licences are in research/sources.json and research/retrieval.json [4–6].

I anchored locations to OPW gauges on the named rivers, retaining gauge coordinates separately from model selectors. Initial nearest cells at Fermoy, Bandon and Athlone produced implausible ten-year means below 1 m³/s; Ennis also needed investigation. A 3-by-3 neighbouring-cell comparison and published river-flow references supported revised selections. Table 1 records all eight locations, while Table 2 records individual plausibility checks. Different reference periods, upstream/downstream locations and statistics limit these comparisons. They establish geographical and order-of-magnitude plausibility, not exact channel matching or calibration. A roughly 5 km model cannot resolve property flooding or local defences.

The history covers 1 January 2016–31 December 2025: 3,653 consecutive daily values per town, 29,224 overall, without missing, nonnumeric or negative flows. P95/P99 use linear interpolation at zero-based rank (n−1)p, equivalent to PERCENTILE.INC. Decisions use unrounded values. Events are consecutive runs at or above P99 within October–March winters 2016/17–2024/25, resetting at winter boundaries. First-four versus last-four counts exclude the middle winter. A second PowerShell method independently reproduced every P99 and winter count. Only the stored summary enters routine questions; ten years of history are not downloaded again.

Warnings use type, level, onset, expiry and regions, with original headline/description and issue/update times retained. FIPS matching includes Roscommon for Athlone and Carrick-on-Shannon. Orange/red rain overlapping the next 48 hours raises the level once; yellow and wind alone do not. An empty valid feed means no listed warnings; a failed read prevents a new plan. The Team Sheet has the prescribed eleven headers, eight starter rows and operational notes. Public CSV and native Sheet rendering were verified, and direct lecturer Editor access was confirmed by the student.

The final brain is Groq Free, openai/gpt-oss-120b. The cheaper 20B model was tested first but made explanation errors. Deterministic code calculates thresholds, levels and constrained crew allocation; the model explains the evidence. A server scope guard refuses excluded property questions without model generation. Groq's published paid-equivalent rates are $0.15/million input and $0.60/million output tokens; measured usage appears in Task 3 [3]. Staying on Free without enabling paid billing is the charge backstop. Rate limits restrict usage, not future paid-account spending. The working key is bound server-side as GROQ_API_KEY and never sent to the browser or committed; the account owner confirmed it is a Cloudflare runtime Secret.

Architecture: Duty Officer → GitHub Pages → Cloudflare Worker → fresh Team Sheet, Open-Meteo forecast and Met Éireann warnings → deterministic plan plus stored history → Groq explanation → human decision. The Worker holds the key. Each question refreshes all three operational sources. The Duty Officer records the final decision by 16:00; assigned barriers are due before 18:00. The Area Manager uses event frequency to prioritise investigation, not to approve a defence design.
